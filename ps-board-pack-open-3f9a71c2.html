Why support spend increased

Spend followed volume. The largest client launches in company history landed in July and August, and demand roughly doubled every month.

Suggested storyline for the slide
  1. The largest client launches in company history landed in July and August: 57k–79k new patients a week, enrolled base 0.9M → 1.2M in six weeks, demand doubling each month.
  2. Spend followed volume: $2.1M a month in January, about $3.0M from February, $3.2M in July. August to follow.
  3. The plan assumed 34% growth for August; actual growth was 117%. Capacity was bought at peak, on short notice.
  4. Punchline: June to July spend rose 7% while calls more than doubled. The cost of under-planning was service, not dollars.
Visual: spend per month (Jan–Jul) beside inbound contacts per week by channel.
  • Spend. Vendor spend: $2.1M a month in January, about $3.0M from February, $3.2M in July. August, the first full surge month, follows when finance closes it.
  • Volume. Calls offered doubled from July to August (247k → 536k). Email tickets went from 17k a month in June to about 190k in August. Chat launched in August and reached 100k sessions a week. About 535k patient contacts a month in total.
  • Why. Patients. The five largest clients added 57k–79k patients a week; their enrolled base grew from 0.90M to 1.23M between Jul 26 and Sep 6. Embody went from 102k to 395k patients and its weekly contacts rose 8×; the AltRx brands grew 20×. Contacts per active patient held near 0.3–0.4 a week, so demand grew because enrollment did. The plan assumed 34% growth for August; actual was 117%, so capacity was added at peak, on short notice.

Sources: finance BPO expense analyses (June, July) · Amazon Connect · Zoho Desk · Healthie (signups and enrolled base). Weekly signups and contacts per patient in the appendix.

What we did to fix it

We hired, launched chat, and put AI on every channel. Service broke for three weeks and recovered while volume kept climbing.

Suggested storyline for the slide (likely two slides: the fix, then the automation)
  1. Name the failure first: three bad weeks (Aug 3–23), abandonment up to 31%, Embody waits of 26 minutes with daily peaks near three hours.
  2. Three moves: hired (+65% inbound agents in eight weeks), launched chat (0 → 100k sessions a week), put AI on every channel.
  3. Recovery while volume kept climbing: 66% of calls reach an agent, abandonment 6%, average wait under 2 minutes. Quality held: agent ratings 4.4–4.5 of 5.
  4. Code Yellow on email: nothing through Aug 9, then 39% of inbound email closed by AI in the week of Aug 31. Target 60%. Voice and chat self-service live on Embody and AltRx.
  5. Contacts handled without an agent: 550 → 27,100 a week in six weeks, net of re-contacts (10% of inbound; 15% before netting). Voice AI lane credited at one-third until audited.
  6. Money: about $0.34M a month today versus no AI, about $7.7M a year at target. Bills fall as vendor staffing is adjusted.
Visual: the answered-calls chart (wait above, abandonment below, Embody highlighted); for automation, the deflection chart and the email chart.
  • Hired. Inbound agents grew about 65% in eight weeks, from about 850 to 1,400, at the vendors serving the launches. Staffing is now planned against forecast peaks.
  • Launched chat. Embody chat went live Aug 11, bot first. Within four weeks it handled about 100k sessions a week (62k reaching the queue) and became Embody's largest channel at about 60% of contacts. Human-answered Embody email fell from 28k to 8k a week and the open backlog is down about 60% from peak. AltRx is next.
  • Put AI on every channel. Code Yellow's email AI classifies every inbound email, closes the routine ones, and routes anything clinical, legal, or distressed to a person. Voice self-service answers order-status calls on Embody and AltRx; the Embody chat bot answers first. Contacts handled without an agent went from about 550 a week in July to 27,100 in the week of Aug 31, net of re-contacts.
  • Result. Calls reaching an agent: 39% of inbound at the low point → 66%. Abandonment 31% → 6%. Embody's average wait 26 minutes (daily peaks near three hours) → about 2 minutes. The last three weeks ran at peak volume with answers in minutes.
  • Quality held. Embody's agent rating held at 4.4–4.5 of 5 on about 9,700 surveys a week. MedVi finished at 81% answered within 30 seconds with 2% abandonment. AltRx absorbed 20× voice growth at sub-40-second answers in five of six weeks. XMD's answer speed went from 47 to 13 seconds and repeat contacts from 4.0 to 1.2 per patient on 18% more volume.

Sources: vendor headcount sheets and rosters · Amazon Connect, including post-call surveys · Zoho Desk. Headcount and chat charts in the appendix.

Automation: Code Yellow on email, self-service on voice and chat

Code Yellow's email AI is the largest piece and reached scale in mid-August; voice and chat self-service are live on Embody and AltRx. A contact resolved before it reaches an agent is the best experience and the cheapest. Lower vendor bills follow only as vendor staffing is adjusted to the lower workload, which is underway.

Code Yellow: email

  • What it is. Every inbound email is sorted into 16 categories, about 80% within 30 minutes. Routine ones are answered and closed; anything clinical, legal, or distressed goes to a person. An escalation-and-distress sweep runs every two hours.
  • Ramp. Almost nothing through Aug 9, then 12k, 22k, 16k, and 19k closes a week: 39% of inbound email in the week of Aug 31.
  • Effect. Human-answered email fell 61% while inbound email fell 14%. Target: 60% of inbound by mid-2027.

Voice and chat self-service

  • Chat. The Embody bot answers first and resolves about 14% of sessions, 11% after re-contacts. AltRx is next Staged.
  • Voice. Callers identified and intent captured on about 85% of Embody and AltRx calls; order-status calls answered without an agent, tracking link texted mid-call. Live on Embody and, since Sep 1, AltRx Live; Trinity, Lean Meds, and Next Meds follow. Counted conservatively until a resolution audit is done.
  • Money, all channels. About $0.34M a month today versus no AI, rising to about $0.64M a month ($7.7M a year) at target. Vendor bills fall only as staffing is adjusted to the lower workload, part of the vendor review.

Sources: Code Yellow dashboard (Zoho Analytics) for email · Amazon Connect for voice and chat · PS cost model. Counting rules and weekly tables in the appendix.

Volume, staffing, spend

Demand more than tripled. Headcount rose about 65% and spend 7%. Agents handled 75% more contacts each and AI took 15% of demand. The cost was three bad weeks of service; patient ratings held.

Suggested storyline for the slide
  1. The tradeoff in one line: demand 3.2×, agents +65%, spend +7% (June to July), contacts per agent +75%, AI 15% of demand.
  2. What it cost: three bad weeks of service; in the worst, 9% of Embody calls answered within 30 seconds against an 80% target.
  3. What comes next: $5.7M a year of modeled savings at today's demand, $10.5M with plan growth; cost +27% while demand +86%.
  4. Beyond cost: the save desk, winback, and refund integrity as measured levers.
  5. Close on the January 1 commitments and the named risks.
Visual: the early-July → early-September change bars, then savings by initiative.
  • What grew. Inbound contacts went from 81k to 263k a week (3.2×). Contacts handled by agents went from 56k to 162k (2.9×).
  • What absorbed it. Inbound agents +65% (850 → 1,400). Contacts per agent +75% (66 → 116 a week). Contacts handled without an agent: under 1% → 15%. Vendor spend +7% June to July (August to follow).
  • What it cost. Service. Abandonment ran 16–31% for three weeks before settling at 5–6%. In Embody's worst week, 9% of calls were answered within 30 seconds against an 80% target and 36% abandoned (46% on the worst day).
  • What comes next. Five automation initiatives are modeled to save about $5.7M a year by June 2027 at today's demand, about $10.5M if demand grows with finance's plan (+86% by December); with that growth, cost rises 27% while demand rises 86%. At today's demand: voice self-service $1.8M, AI QA $1.6M, Code Yellow email AI $1.2M, chat bot $0.6M, back-office automation $0.5M.
  • By January 1. Pod model fully live. 100% AI QA scoring on voice, chat, and email. Voice self-service on every major brand. Agents on the largest book see the patient's journey and likely reason before hello. Save desk piloting with a measured save rate.
  • Beyond cost. Two revenue levers start this quarter: a save desk for cancel-intent contacts (modeled floor about $2.2M a year) and refund integrity against about $5M a year of leakage already surfaced. Winback has relaunched; the 2025 pilot saved 59% of lapsed patients worked, and each 1,000 worked a month is modeled at $2.6–5.3M a year.
  • Still open. Chat answer speed (30–37% of Embody chats within 30 seconds against an 80% target); chat agents are being added now. August spend actuals from finance. The vendor contract review, starting with the largest vendor.
  • Risks. Demand can outrun the AI ramps, as August showed; capacity triggers now sit on the forecast. One bad automated experience costs more than ten good ones earn, so deflection is counted net of re-contacts, guardrails fail closed, and AI interactions get QA-scored like humans. Much of this was built by hand, fast; the operations hire and documentation make it durable.

A0 · Reference charts

A1 · Definitions and scope

  • Inbound calls (weekly funnel) = inbound legs deduplicated by contact. Outcomes: reached an agent (agent-connected); self-served, verified (the call carries an order-status-answered or completion marker); AI lane ended call, unverified (the AI voice lane stamped "complete" and disconnected; a 12-hour re-contact audit on the Aug 31–Sep 5 cohort found 75% of these callers called back, 66% for the same need, versus 14% / 13% after an agent conversation, so this slice is credited at one-third in the net figures and not claimed on the chart); took a callback (caller accepted a callback offer; the return call is a separate outbound leg and is not shown); abandoned in queue; hung up in menu / message / error (left after more than 30 seconds in the menu, heard a closed-hours or redirect message the flow then ended — median 16 seconds, almost all non-Embody — or hit a system error prompt); junk (telecom cancels and hang-ups within 30 seconds — misdials and robocalls). Before Aug 10 no self-service path existed beyond a small order-status script, which is why the verified segment is a few hundred calls a week in July. Monthly offered (247k / 536k) counts every contact including no-agent legs, which is why it exceeds the weekly totals summed.
  • Email received = tickets created in the client patient inboxes in the week; human-answered = tickets receiving a first human reply in the week; human replies = all outgoing human replies (≈1.6 per ticket, falling to ~1.0 on Embody as chat took the back-and-forth).
  • AI resolution (email) = an inbound-email ticket carrying the Code Yellow dashboard's HeyRevia close timestamp, i.e. closed by the AI (the Code Yellow team's own "AI resolutions" definition, from the "Code Yellow All Tickets" table in Zoho Analytics). Net excludes tickets the patient later reopened by replying on the thread (34–39% of AI closes in Aug). A random sample (n=120, Aug 18) found 88% of AI-closed patient tickets carried an AI-authored reply.
  • AI share of inbound (37–39%, late Aug): two measurements agree — the cost model's planning coefficient (1 − human answers ÷ inbound on the email-brain feed) and the direct first-party count (AI-closed tickets ÷ inbound email created, by week: 22% wk Aug 10, 33% wk Aug 17, 37% wk Aug 24).
  • Chat sessions = chat contacts started from the Embody widget or routed to the MedVi chat queue (the shared general chat queue, ~7–9k agent chats a week, has no client attribution and is excluded). Resolved by bot = journey stage "deflected" or "deflected silent"; net deflection subtracts same-person returns within 12 hours on the same topic. "Ended before queue" = sessions that ended after a bot authentication failure, outside staffed hours, or mid-flow without enqueueing.
  • Chat concurrency = 2 sessions per agent on the TTEC and Response chat routing profiles, 1 on Narith (Aug 14 routing configuration). Throughput per agent-hour has not yet been measured for Embody chat; the "roughly twice" claim is the configuration, not a measurement.
  • Why deflection savings are less than linear: the AI absorbs the quickest contacts first, so residual human mail and calls are harder. Email throughput is modeled to glide from 2.4 to 2.0 replies per hour and residual call handle time drifts up; the cost model carries this (+$0.93M of 2027 cost vs an uncorrected model). Planning coefficient for AI email share: ~10% (Jul) → 37–39% (late Aug) → 55% (Dec) → 60% ceiling (Jun 2027), since ~48% of inbound needs human escalation today.
  • Should-cost = the demand-driven model priced at planning-standard occupancy (65%) and shrinkage (15%) using measured handle times and contract/derived rates. Actual minus should-cost is the efficiency premium, not model error. The $5.28 per subscriber is August's modeled true cost (incl. TTEC, excl. the Fridays entry) divided by June's active-subscriber count; a September actual-cost per subscriber needs finance's August invoices.
  • Front-line agents = reps on the three voice/chat/email vendors' rosters (TTEC, Narith, Response). NextVas works in the patient CRM (empaneled care) and Booth is outside the surge scope; both appear only in the 5-vendor totals. Headcounts are whole people. Inbound-only counts on a single basis (excluding retention, back office, refund ops, VPX, Day 0 for Response; PAMS and prior auth for Narith; temp QA/trainer for TTEC; only agents with a start date before Sep 1): Response 484 (Jul 6 headcount sheet) → 662 (Sep 1 roster); Narith 192 (June business review) → 293 (Sep 1 roster); TTEC 174 (start of July, per Ryan) → 444 (Sep 1 roster); total 850 → 1,399 (+65%). Contacts handled per inbound agent per week: 56,358 ÷ 850 = 66 (week of Jul 20) → 145,139 ÷ 1,399 = 104 (week of Aug 24).
  • Patient ratings = Amazon Connect post-call surveys on a 1–5 scale. "Representative Satisfaction" is the agent rating and "Call Experience" the call rating. Embody: 5.0k–9.8k surveys a week, Jul 27 – Sep 6; agent rating 4.41–4.53, call experience 4.13–4.31.
  • Live = in production today. Staged = built or decided, not yet in production.
