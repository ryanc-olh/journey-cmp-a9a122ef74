Scope — who counts
Everything on this page is restricted to contacts that reached a queue. Patients who resolved themselves in the IVR, or who hung up before ever reaching a queue, are excluded from every scenario.

In scope

  • Calls that entered the hold queue — the patient waited
  • Callbacks we placed — these re-enter the queue and we dial out
  • Patients who reached a queue by either path

Excluded

  • 991 patients who never reached any queue
  • 8,525 inbound calls that ended in the IVR — self-service, deflection, or hang-up before the queue
  • Non-queued contact types (survey legs, transfers with no queue, monitoring)
Of the 7,188 patients who dialled Embody that day, 6,197 reached a queue and are in scope. The 8,525 excluded calls include the first leg of patients who did reach a queue later via a callback — excluding the call does not exclude the patient.
The flow
A patient reaches the queue, then either waits and connects, gives up, or takes a callback. Callbacks go back into the queue and we dial out — which may or may not connect.

All six scenarios above treat a callback as a valid way to reach the queue, so the 3,567 callback-only patients are in scope. A stricter reading counts only patients who actually waited in the hold queue (2,630). Shown separately because it changes the denominator, not the method.

Callbacks still count as a valid way to be served here — they just no longer count as a way to enter the population. 1,601 of the 2,630 (60.9%) spoke to someone.
Why D reads roughly twice C. It removes 3,567 patients from the denominator, and 3,420 of them (95.9%) were successfully served. Dropping a well-served group leaves a smaller, worse-performing population. D measures the health of the hold queue; C measures whether a patient got help.

Method & known limits
  • One day only. Tue 2026-08-04, Central Time. Not a trailing average. The client dashboard's headline card is a trailing 7-day figure, which deduplicates patients across the whole week and therefore reads lower than any single bad day — 12% for Jul 29–Aug 4 against 19.0% for this Tuesday alone.
  • "No agent" is not the same as "abandoned." The 2,214 figure counts held legs with no agent_connected_to_agent_timestamp. It includes patients who left the hold queue deliberately to take a callback. Every call-based and leg-based scenario inherits this; the patient-based ones do not, because those patients appear as served.
  • IDV / queue composition unverified. The in-scope filter is "carries a queue timestamp", which is queue-agnostic. Whether any of the 3,094 held legs sit in an identity-verification queue rather than an agent queue has not been checked. If such a queue exists, the hold-queue counts here are overstated.
  • Mirror drift. The Connect→Zoho mirror is refreshed through the day. This page's leg counts were read before the 14:44 build, which recorded 8,870 legs and 6,089 connected for the same day — a four-leg difference that does not change any rate above at one decimal.
  • Voice only. Chat and email are excluded entirely. Embody runs no chat.
  • Patient identity is patient_id on the Connect record, deduplicated within the day. A patient with no patient_id cannot be counted in the patient-based scenarios; voice linkage runs near 100%.
