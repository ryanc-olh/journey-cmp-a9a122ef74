A callback does two separate jobs, and each scenario answers them independently. Keeping them apart is what makes the table above read cleanly:
  Job 1 — entry. Does having a callback placed put a patient into the population? C says yes. D says no. Read this in the population column.
  Job 2 — service. Does a callback mean the patient was served? The "1" scenarios say only if it connected. The "2" scenarios say always. Read this in the abandon column.
A scenario can answer yes to entry and only if connected to service — that combination is C1, which is why C1 includes patients who got callbacks, both in its population and among its abandons.
A2 cannot be computed from data we hold today — it needs a join that has never been run, and is left blank rather than estimated (see its block below). C and D use the identical abandon rule and differ only on job 1: C lets a callback bring a patient into the population, D does not.
Scope — who is counted at all
Every scenario is restricted to contacts that reached a queue. Anyone who resolved themselves in the IVR, or hung up before reaching a queue, is excluded throughout.

In scope

  • Calls that entered the hold queue — the patient waited
  • Callbacks we placed — these re-enter the queue and we dial out
  • Patients who reached a queue by either path

Excluded

  • 991 patients who never reached any queue
  • 8,525 inbound calls that ended in the IVR — self-service, deflection, or hang-up before the queue
  • Records with no queue at all — survey legs, monitoring, un-queued transfers
Of the 7,188 patients who dialled Embody that day, 6,197 reached a queue. Note the asymmetry: those 8,525 excluded calls include the first call of patients who did reach a queue later via a callback — excluding a call does not exclude its patient.

Two open questions
Both are unresolved, both are material, and one query answers both.
1. Is there an identity-verification queue inside the 3,094?
The scope filter is "the record carries a queue timestamp", which is queue-agnostic — queue_name has never been inspected. If IDV or another verification step routes through its own queue, those records are currently counted as hold-queue waits, and every hold-queue figure on this page is overstated — A1, B1, B2 and both D scenarios. C1 and C2 would be least affected, since a patient who cleared IDV and then waited appears once either way.

Method & known limits
  • Source. Amazon Connect contact records read through the Zoho Analytics mirror (Amazon Connect Logs Adj), channel = VOICE, Org Name in Embody / White Coat - Embody, DATE(initiation_timestamp_cst) = '2026-08-04'. A record is queued when it carries queue_enqueue_timestamp and connected when it carries agent_connected_to_agent_timestamp. Callbacks are initiation_method = 'CALLBACK'.
  • "No agent" does not always mean the patient abandoned. The 2,214 counts held records with no agent connect, and includes patients who left the hold queue deliberately to take a callback — those are not abandons in any ordinary sense. Every call- and record-based scenario inherits this overstatement; the people-based ones do not, because those patients appear as served.
  • One day only. Not a trailing average. Any multi-day window deduplicates patients across the whole window, so a weekly figure reads lower than a bad day inside it — someone who fails Tuesday and gets through Wednesday counts as served for the week. Rates from different window lengths are not comparable.
  • Mirror drift. The Connect→Zoho mirror refreshes through the day, so the same query can return slightly different counts when re-run. A later read of this day gave 8,870 records and 6,089 connected against the 8,866 / 6,091 used here — four records, and no change to any rate at one decimal.
  • Patient identity is patient_id, deduplicated within the day. A record with no patient_id cannot enter the people-based scenarios; voice linkage runs near 100%.
  • Voice only. Chat and email are excluded entirely. Embody runs no chat.
  • Arithmetic is machine-checked. Every identity and every rate printed here is asserted by an audit script at build time; the page is not published if one fails.
